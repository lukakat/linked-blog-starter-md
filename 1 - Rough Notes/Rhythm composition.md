Staggered introduction of the parts to slowly build texture every two bars.
Off-beat syncopation in the Rubicorn can with the Tupperware on beats 2 and 4 due to jazz influences.
The paper rips from the first beat to the third, leaving the fourth beat free for the car keys.
Every two bars, once the instruments have all been introduced, they change their rhythm to keep it interesting.
The Tupperware part could be more creative; it is currently four beats per bar. Consider including some triplets.
The car keys are placed on the second beat but have a long sustain compared to the other percussive sounds.
The Tupperware also needs to be clasped and reclasped to provide some tonal difference.
There is a break at bar 17 with all the sustained sounds the percussion can make.
Bar 18 features an exploded drum kit part in quadruple compound time, written in triplets with different accents on the paper.
Then, four on-beat hits are passed around the four instruments while a 4:3 polyrhythm is passed around the others until it returns to beat one.
At bar 25, seven beats are placed over the 4/4 time signature, making the hits land on decrementing beats each time until beat one, where the piece ends.
I was originally going to write more exploded drum parts that flowed through each other with transitionary sections. However, I think the difficulty of counting and the gimmicks involved are better, though they might not be more musical.
